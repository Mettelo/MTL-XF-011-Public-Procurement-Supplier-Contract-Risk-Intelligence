# Project Brief

## Engagement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Project | MTL-XF-011 — Public Procurement Spend, Supplier Concentration & Contract Risk Intelligence |
| Client | Confidential Public Sector Commercial & Procurement Directorate |
| Sector | Public Sector / Commercial / Procurement |
| Duration | 8 weeks |
| Team size | 6–8 |
| Sponsor | Commercial Director |
| Primary data | Contracts Finder Notices — June 2026 (Open Contracting Data Standard flattened CSV) |

## 1. Client Situation

The organisation manages a large portfolio of procurement notices and supplier relationships, but senior leaders cannot reliably identify supplier concentration, fragmented purchasing, contract expiry exposure, SME participation or opportunities for commercial intervention from one governed view.

The issue is not simply lack of reporting. Different stakeholders currently interpret the available data differently, definitions are not sufficiently governed, analytical evidence is not consistently translated into operational decisions, and leadership needs a product that can be maintained after the project team leaves.

## 2. Commission

The multidisciplinary team has been commissioned to create a governed, reproducible decision-intelligence solution answering:

> **Where should commercial leadership intervene first to reduce supplier dependency, prepare for contract expiry, consolidate fragmented demand and improve procurement outcomes?**

## 3. Business Questions

1. Which suppliers account for the greatest concentration of awarded value and notice volume?
2. Which categories or buyers show fragmented purchasing that may warrant consolidation?
3. Which contracts or awards create upcoming expiry/re-procurement pressure?
4. How does SME participation vary by buyer, category or contract size?
5. Where do data-quality issues prevent reliable commercial decisions?
6. Which indicators should form an executive supplier/contract risk view?

## 4. Required Delivery Approach

Teams must work through:

**Discovery → Requirements → Governance → Data Acquisition → Engineering → Data Quality → Analysis → Decision Framework → BI/Product → Assurance/UAT → Executive Recommendation → Handover**

Roles are interdependent. Requirements should influence data modelling; governance rules should influence engineering; approved metric definitions should control BI calculations; analytical findings should be tested before becoming recommendations.

## 5. Cross-Functional Roles

| Role | Expected contribution |
|---|---|
| Business Analyst | Lead discovery, stakeholder mapping, requirements, user stories, decision rules and acceptance criteria. |
| Data Governance Analyst | Define ownership, glossary, quality rules, lineage, metadata and authoritative procurement fields. |
| Data Engineer | Build reproducible ingestion and transformation of daily OCDS CSV releases. |
| Data Analyst / Commercial Analyst | Analyse supplier concentration, procurement patterns, contract values, timing and SME participation. |
| BI Developer | Build governed semantic measures and the commercial decision-support dashboard. |
| Data Quality / Assurance Analyst | Test reconciliations, duplicate handling, field completeness and output controls. |

Individuals may cover more than one role, but responsibilities and evidence must remain explicit.

## 6. Success Criteria

The final solution should demonstrate:

- a clearly evidenced business problem and decision;
- traceable requirements and acceptance criteria;
- documented data ownership, definitions, lineage and quality rules;
- reproducible acquisition/transformation;
- defensible analysis and approved KPI definitions;
- transparent decision/prioritisation logic;
- stakeholder-appropriate BI or decision-support output;
- assurance/UAT evidence;
- operational and technical handover;
- visible contribution from multiple team members.

## 7. Review Panel Challenge

The Mettelo review panel may challenge assumptions, definitions, data quality, stakeholder requirements, metric logic, decision thresholds, governance, maintainability, recommendations and whether the product genuinely answers the commissioned decision.
